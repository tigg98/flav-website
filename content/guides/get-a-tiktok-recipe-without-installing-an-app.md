# Can I get the recipe from a TikTok link without installing an app?

Short answer: yes, when the recipe is written in the video's caption. You can copy the caption into a note by hand, or paste the video's link into a web page that reads the caption and writes the recipe out for you. We make one of those pages, flav.app/try. Either way you get the recipe once and nothing keeps it for you, so put it somewhere you'll find it when you cook.

We make Flav, a recipe app for iPhone. This guide is about what you can do before you install anything.

## What you're trying to get

A recipe you can cook from has three parts: the ingredients with amounts, the steps in order, and a link back to the video for the one bit you need to see. The link on its own only gives you the video. The job is to turn the video into text, once.

## Way 1: copy the caption by hand

If the creator wrote the recipe in the caption, the text is already there. Copy it into a note, put the video's link at the top, then put the ingredients in a list and number the steps. [This guide has the steps](/guides/how-to-save-a-recipe-from-tiktok).

It costs nothing and needs no new app. The slow part is the tidying. Captions are written for scrolling, not cooking, so amounts sit in the middle of sentences and steps run together.

## Way 2: paste the link into a web page

A web page that reads TikTok links does the copying and the tidying for you. Ours is [flav.app/try](/try). Here's how it goes:

1. Copy the link to the TikTok.
2. Open flav.app/try in your browser and paste the link.
3. The page reads the video's caption and shows the recipe it finds: the ingredients and the steps, as text.

It usually takes a few seconds. A long caption can take about 15.

A few things to know before you use it:

- **It's built for TikTok links.** Instagram links aren't supported yet.
- **It works from what's written.** If the creator wrote "a splash of soy", that's what you'll see. It doesn't make up amounts.
- **It's limited to three tries a day.** That's enough to test it on the recipes you care about.
- **It shows the recipe, it doesn't keep it.** The result is on the page in your browser. There's no cookbook on the web.

## Where both ways stop

Both ways read the caption. Some creators say the recipe out loud and write nothing down, or the caption is just a title and hashtags. Copying a caption like that gets you nothing, and flav.app/try will tell you there's no written recipe for it to read. It reads the caption, not the video itself.

The recipe may still be written somewhere else. [This guide covers where to look](/guides/tiktok-recipe-not-in-caption).

## Keep what you get

A recipe you can't find again isn't saved. Once you have the text, from either way:

- Paste it into a note, with the video's link at the top.
- Give the note a name you'd search for, like the dish, not "recipe".
- Fix the amounts after the first time you cook it.

If you only cook from a video now and then, that's all you need.

## When an app starts to make sense

A note per recipe works for a handful. It gets harder when you save a few every week. That's the point where a recipe app is worth having:

- Your recipes sit in one cookbook, not scattered across notes.
- A recipe's ingredients can go onto a grocery list.
- You can cook one step at a time with timers, not from a block of text.
