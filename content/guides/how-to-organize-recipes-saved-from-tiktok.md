# How do I organize recipes I saved from TikTok?

Short answer: don't try to organize everything. Go through your saves once, pull out the recipes you'd genuinely cook, turn those into text with ingredients and steps, and sort them by when you'd make them, not by cuisine. Leave the rest in Favorites as entertainment. A cookbook of 40 recipes you use beats a Favorites list of 400 you scroll past.

We build Flav, a recipe app for exactly this. The method below works with any app, or with Notes.

## Why Favorites gets messy

TikTok Favorites is a pile of thumbnails. You can sort them into collections, but each one is still a video you have to open, play and pause to cook from. Nothing in it is searchable by ingredient. You can't look for "chicken thighs" and find the three you saved. And the pile grows every time you scroll, so it only gets harder to use.

The fix isn't better folders for videos. It's turning the few that matter into recipes.

## Step 1: one pass, three piles

Set aside half an hour and go through your Favorites with a simple rule for each video:

- **Would cook this month.** Keep, and turn it into a recipe now.
- **Might cook someday.** Leave it in a TikTok collection called "Someday". Don't spend time on it.
- **Fun to watch.** Leave it where it is, or unsave it. Not every food video is a recipe you need.

Be strict about the first pile. If you haven't made it in the six months since you saved it, it's a "someday".

## Step 2: turn the keepers into text

Each keeper needs ingredients with amounts, steps in order, and the video link. You can copy the caption by hand, or share each video to a recipe app that pulls it out for you. [This guide covers both](/guides/how-to-save-a-recipe-from-tiktok).

Once a recipe is text, you can search it, put it on a grocery list, and read it with one glance while you cook. That's the point of this step.

## Step 3: sort by when you'd cook it

Sorting by cuisine sounds tidy and rarely helps. When you're deciding what to make, you're asking "what can I make on a Tuesday in 30 minutes?", not "what's Thai?". Groups that tend to get used:

- Weeknight (under 30 minutes)
- Weekend or project
- Meal prep and lunches
- Having people over
- Tried and loved

That last one matters most. When you cook something and like it, move it there and fix the amounts while you remember. Over a few months it becomes your actual cookbook.

## Step 4: keep it small on purpose

New saves go straight to a recipe, not to Favorites, if you mean to cook them. Every few months, look at what you haven't made and let some go. A recipe box you trust is one where everything in it is something you'd make.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I organize TikTok Favorites into folders?**
A: Yes. TikTok lets you create collections inside Favorites and move saved videos into them. That sorts the videos, but each one is still a video to play and pause. For recipes you'll cook, it helps to save the ingredients and steps as text.

**Q: What's the best way to organize recipes from TikTok?**
A: Keep only what you'd cook soon, turn those into text recipes with ingredients and steps, and sort them by when you'd make them, like weeknight, weekend and meal prep. Keep a "tried and loved" group for the ones that worked.

**Q: Can ChatGPT organize my saved TikTok recipes?**
A: It can't see your TikTok Favorites. If you paste in a list of dish names or recipe text, it can suggest groups or tidy the recipes. You'd still need a place to keep them, like Notes or a recipe app.

**Q: How many recipes should I keep?**
A: Fewer than you think. A few dozen you'd actually make is more useful than hundreds you scroll past. Review every few months and let go of what you haven't cooked.
