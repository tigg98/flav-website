# What do I do when a TikTok recipe has no measurements?

Short answer: treat the first cook as the measuring. Check the amounts aren't written somewhere else, then read the video for clues, start low on anything you can't take back out, taste as you go, and write down what you used. After one cook you have a real recipe. We make Flav, a recipe app, and it keeps amounts the way the creator wrote them, so this part is still yours to do.

## Why the amounts go missing

A lot of people who cook on camera cook by eye, and a short video has no room for a full recipe. So the caption says "soy sauce, honey, garlic" with no numbers, or "season to taste", or "a splash". That's fine for watching. It's hard to shop for and hard to repeat.

## First, check they aren't written somewhere else

Before you guess, read the whole caption, not just the first line. Then check the other places a creator might keep the written version. [This guide lists them](/guides/tiktok-recipe-not-in-caption). A recipe on the creator's own site usually has the amounts the video skipped.

## Read the video for clues

If the numbers aren't anywhere, the video still shows you a lot. Watch it once just for amounts, and pause when you need to:

- **Packages.** A whole can, a full block, one bag. A package is a measurement.
- **Tools.** A teaspoon and a tablespoon look different on screen. So does a measuring cup.
- **Counts.** Cloves of garlic, eggs, chicken thighs. Count them.
- **The pan.** How full it is tells you how much liquid went in.
- **The plates.** How many servings come out at the end tells you the size of the whole thing.

Write a number next to every ingredient, and put a question mark by the ones you guessed.

## Start low on what you can't undo

You can always add more. You can't take it out. For these, put in about half of what you think you saw, taste, then add:

- Salt, and salty things like soy sauce and stock.
- Chili and hot sauce.
- Vinegar and citrus juice.
- Sugar and honey in a sauce.

Onions, vegetables and most fresh herbs are forgiving. A bit more or less won't ruin dinner.

## Be stricter with baking

Cooking on the stove forgives a guess. Baking mostly doesn't, because the amounts of flour, liquid, sugar and raising agent depend on each other. If a baking video gives no amounts, find a written recipe for the same thing and use the video for technique: how thick the batter looks, how they shape it, what color it comes out.

## Times and heat get cut too

Short videos edit out the waiting, so "cook the onions" might be ten minutes that passed in a second. Go by what the food looks like in the video, not by a number: the color of the onions, how thick the sauce is, how hard it's bubbling. Note how long it took on your stove.

## Write it down the night you cook

This is the step that turns a guess into a recipe. While you still remember, replace "a splash" with what you used, add the time it really took, and note anything you'd change. The second time you cook it, you won't be guessing.

## FAQ

**Q: Why don't TikTok recipes have measurements?**
A: Many creators cook by eye, and a short video has no room for a full recipe. Sometimes the amounts are written on the creator's own site. Check there before you guess.

**Q: How do I work out amounts from a cooking video?**
A: Look for packages, measuring tools, things you can count, and how full the pan is. Write a number for each ingredient, start with less salt and spice than you think, and taste as you go.

**Q: Can a recipe app fill in the missing measurements?**
A: An app that reads the caption can only save what's written there. Flav keeps amounts as the creator wrote them and doesn't guess the ones that are missing. You can fix the recipe yourself after the first cook.

**Q: Does guessing work for baking?**
A: Not as well as it does on the stove. In baking the amounts depend on each other, so use a written recipe for the same bake and watch the video for technique.
