# How do I turn a cooking video into a grocery list?

Short answer: get the ingredient list out of the video first, as text, then cross off what you already have and shop from what's left. You can copy the ingredients from the caption into Notes or Reminders by hand. Or save the video to a recipe app that pulls out the ingredients with quantities and adds them to a grocery list for you. We make one, Flav, and we'll cover both ways.

## Step 1: find the ingredients

The ingredients are usually in one of three places:

- **The caption.** Most food creators list them, sometimes with amounts, sometimes without. Tap to expand it.
- **The comments.** If the caption is short, the creator may have pinned the recipe in a comment.
- **The creator's site.** "Recipe in bio" means a link on their profile, usually to a full recipe page.

If none of those has it and the creator only says it out loud, watch the video once with captions on and write the ingredients down as they appear. [More on that here](/guides/tiktok-recipe-not-in-caption).

## Step 2: get amounts, not just names

A grocery list that says "chicken, garlic, cream" is how you end up with one clove of garlic and a recipe that wants eight. Write the quantity next to every item. If the creator didn't give one, guess based on what you saw and note that it's a guess.

Watch for the ingredients that sneak in without being listed: oil for the pan, salt, the splash of stock at the end. Short videos skip them all the time.

## Step 3: check what you have

Go through the list in your kitchen before you leave. Cross off what's already there, and check you have enough of it. "Have soy sauce" isn't the same as "have half a cup of soy sauce".

## Step 4: sort it for the store

Group the list by where things are: produce, meat and fish, dairy, dry goods, frozen. It saves you walking back across the store for the lemon you missed. If you use Reminders on iPhone, a Groceries list can sort items into sections for you.

## Step 5: do it for the week, not the meal

If you're cooking three things from three videos this week, put all three on one list. Combine the duplicates: two recipes that each want an onion means two onions, not "onion" twice. This is where people save the most money and the most trips.

## A few things that go wrong

- **Missing quantities.** If the recipe says "chicken thighs" with no number, decide before you shop.
- **Pantry staples.** Oil, salt and pepper rarely make it into a video's ingredient list. Check you have them.
- **Specialty items.** Gochujang, fish sauce, a specific cheese. Check your store has it before you plan the meal around it, and think about what you'd swap in.

## FAQ

**Q: Is there an app that makes a grocery list from a TikTok?**
A: Yes. Several recipe apps, including Flav, ReciMe, Pestle, Paprika and Honeydew, can save a recipe from a TikTok link and add its ingredients to a grocery list. They read the caption and page, so the ingredients need to be written there.

**Q: Can ChatGPT make a grocery list from a cooking video?**
A: If you paste the recipe or caption into ChatGPT, it can turn it into a sorted grocery list and combine ingredients from several recipes. It can't reliably watch the video, and it won't keep the list on your phone for the store.

**Q: What if the video doesn't list the ingredients?**
A: Check the pinned comment and the creator's bio link for a full recipe. If it's only spoken, watch once with captions on and write them down, with your best guess at amounts.

**Q: How do I share a grocery list with my partner?**
A: In Reminders, share the list with them from the list's share button. In Flav, share with your household and the grocery list is shared too.
