# How do I plan a week of meals from recipes I saved on TikTok?

Short answer: pick three or four saved recipes you'd really cook this week, not seven. Get each one out of the video as text with amounts, put them on the days that fit, and build one grocery list from all of them. Leave the other nights open. We make Flav, a recipe app with a meal plan, and the same method works on paper.

## Why a list of saved videos isn't a plan

A saved video is an idea. A plan answers three things the video doesn't: which recipes, on which nights, and what you need to buy. Without those, you get to 6 pm, scroll your saves, find you're missing half the ingredients, and make something else.

So planning from TikTok is mostly a small amount of sorting, done once, before you shop.

## Step 1: pick fewer than you think

Look at your week before you look at your saves. Which nights are late? Who's home? Then pick three or four recipes:

- One you've made before and liked.
- One or two new ones you're curious about.
- One that makes enough for a second night or for lunches.

Seven new recipes in a week sounds good on Sunday and falls apart by Wednesday. A short plan you finish beats a full one you drop.

## Step 2: make sure each one is written down

Before a recipe goes on the plan, it has to exist as text: ingredients with amounts, and steps in order. You can't shop from a video, and the worst time to learn the creator never wrote the recipe down is when you're hungry.

Copy the caption into a note, or share the video to a recipe app that pulls the recipe out. [This guide covers both](/guides/how-to-save-a-recipe-from-tiktok). If the caption is empty, [look in these places](/guides/tiktok-recipe-not-in-caption), or pick a different recipe for this week.

## Step 3: put recipes on days

Match each recipe to a night:

- **Quick ones on busy nights.** Save the long one for a day you have time.
- **Use what spoils first.** Fish, salad leaves and soft herbs go early in the week.
- **Pair recipes that share an ingredient.** If one needs half a bunch of cilantro, plan something for the other half.
- **Leave gaps.** Leftovers, eggs on toast and eating out are part of a real week.

Write it down somewhere everyone who eats can see it.

## Step 4: one list, one shop

Put the ingredients from every recipe on one grocery list. Add up the doubles: two recipes that each want an onion means two onions. Then walk through your kitchen and cross off what you already have enough of.

[This guide goes through the grocery list step by step](/guides/turn-a-cooking-video-into-a-grocery-list).

## Step 5: look back at the end of the week

Take two minutes when the week is done:

- Keep the recipes you'd make again, and fix the amounts while you remember.
- Let go of the ones that weren't worth it.
- Note anything you planned and didn't cook. It can lead next week's plan, or it's a sign you don't want to make it.

After a few weeks you have a set of meals you know work, and planning gets quicker.

## FAQ

**Q: How many recipes should I plan for a week?**
A: Three or four is plenty for most weeks. Leave the other nights for leftovers, something simple, or eating out. A plan with a new recipe every night is hard to keep up.

**Q: Can I plan meals from saved videos without writing the recipes down?**
A: You can pick the dishes, but you can't shop from a list of videos. A plan needs ingredients with amounts so you can build one grocery list. Get each recipe into text first.

**Q: Is there an app that makes a meal plan from TikTok recipes?**
A: Flav saves recipes from TikTok links and has a meal plan and a grocery list built from a recipe's ingredients. Flav+ adds AI meal planning. Flav is iPhone only.

**Q: What if one of the recipes isn't written down anywhere?**
A: Swap it for one that is, or write it down yourself before the week starts. [This guide covers how](/guides/tiktok-recipe-not-in-caption).
