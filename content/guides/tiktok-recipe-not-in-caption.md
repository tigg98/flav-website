# How do I get the recipe from a TikTok when it isn't in the caption?

Short answer: look in three places before you give up. The pinned or top comment, the link in the creator's bio, and the creator's website or newsletter. If the recipe is only spoken in the video, the honest fix is to write it down yourself while it plays, with captions on. Most recipe apps, ours included, read the caption and the page, so they can't pull a recipe that was never written down.

We build Flav, so we see this a lot. Here's what actually works.

## 1. Read the comments first

Someone always asks "recipe?" and the creator often answers. On TikTok, the creator's reply or a pinned comment sits at or near the top. Tap the comments icon and look for:

- A pinned comment, marked as pinned.
- A reply from the creator to the "recipe please" comment.
- A comment from a viewer who already typed the whole thing out. Check it against the video before you trust it.

If you find it, copy the comment text into a note with the video link.

## 2. Check the link in bio

A lot of creators keep their recipes on their own site and point to it with "recipe in bio" or "link in bio". Tap the creator's name to open their profile, then the link under their bio. It usually opens a link page or their blog. Search it for the dish name.

A recipe on a web page is the easiest kind to save. You can share that page to a recipe app and get a clean result, because a blog recipe is already written with amounts and steps.

## 3. Search the dish and the creator outside TikTok

If the bio link is a dead end, search the web for the dish name plus the creator's name. Creators often post the same recipe on Instagram with a longer caption, on YouTube with a full description, or in a newsletter. The written version is usually more complete than anything in the video.

## 4. Write it down while it plays

When the recipe is only spoken, nothing beats doing it once by hand.

1. Turn on captions. On TikTok, tap the captions button if the video has them, or use your iPhone's Live Captions under Accessibility.
2. Set playback speed to slower if the creator talks fast.
3. Write the ingredients first, in one pass. Then play it again and write the steps.
4. Note the amounts you can see but aren't said: the size of the pan, how much is in the measuring cup.

It takes about ten minutes. You do it once, and then you never have to watch it again to cook.

## 5. Ask the creator

If you can't find it anywhere, ask in the comments, kindly. Creators want people to make their food, and many will post it if enough people ask.

## What recipe apps can and can't do here

Apps that save recipes from TikTok work by reading what's written: the caption, and the text on the video's page. When the recipe is there, they do a good job of turning it into a clean list. When it isn't, there's nothing to read.

Flav 2.0 works this way. Share a TikTok with an empty caption and you'll get a thin recipe, maybe a title and a few ingredients, or nothing. We'd rather show you that than invent amounts. Reading the video itself is something we plan to add in a later version. It isn't in 2.0.

If you've found the recipe on the creator's site, you can still use Flav for it. Share or paste that page's link and you get the same recipe format as any other import.

## Before you cook a recipe from the comments

A recipe typed by a viewer is a guess at what the creator did. Watch the video once with it in front of you and fix anything that doesn't match. Amounts are where these go wrong, especially salt and anything spicy.

## FAQ

**Q: Why is there no recipe in the TikTok caption?**
A: Captions are short, and many creators keep the full recipe on their website or newsletter to bring people there. Others just say it out loud. Check the pinned comment and the link in the creator's bio before anything else.

**Q: Can an app get the recipe from the video itself?**
A: Some apps say they work from a video's spoken words, and results vary. Flav 2.0 doesn't: it reads the caption and the page. If the recipe is only spoken, Flav may return a thin or empty recipe. Reading the video is planned for a later version.

**Q: Can ChatGPT find the recipe for a TikTok?**
A: It can help you search for the creator's written version, and it can tidy up a recipe you've copied from a comment. It can't reliably watch the TikTok for you. Paste it the text you found and ask for an ingredient list and numbered steps.

**Q: Is a recipe from the comments accurate?**
A: Sometimes. A viewer's version is their best guess at what the creator did. Watch the video once with the comment in front of you and correct the amounts before you cook.
