# Why is TikTok Favorites a bad place to keep recipes?

Short answer: Favorites saves the video, and a video isn't a recipe. There's no ingredient list to shop from, no way to search your saves for "chicken thighs", nothing to follow step by step at the stove, and if the creator deletes the video or goes private, your save goes with it. Favorites is good at what it was built for, which is keeping videos you liked. It just wasn't built to be a recipe box.

We make Flav, a recipe app, so we have a view on this. We've tried to be fair.

## What Favorites does well

It's worth saying first: Favorites is free, it's one tap, and it's already where you are. You can sort saves into collections and keep them to yourself. For "I want to watch that again" it's perfect. A lot of the food videos people save aren't really recipes they'll cook, they're entertainment, and Favorites is the right place for those.

The trouble starts when you treat it as the place you'll come back to when you cook.

## Five ways it falls short for recipes

**1. The recipe is still inside the video.** Every time you cook, you open TikTok, find the video, play it, pause it, scrub back. The ingredients and amounts are in the caption or on screen for a second, not in a list.

**2. You can't search what's in it.** Favorites is a grid of thumbnails. If you remember the dish had miso and you saved it in March, you scroll. There's no "show me everything with chickpeas".

**3. There's nothing to shop from.** To make a grocery list, you write it out yourself from the caption or the video. Every time.

**4. Saves can vanish.** If a creator deletes a video, makes their account private, or the video is taken down, it stops playing from your Favorites. Nothing warns you. You find out the night you wanted to make it.

**5. It grows faster than you cook.** Saving is easier than cooking, so the list keeps growing. After a year, the recipes you'd actually make are buried under hundreds you won't. The more you save, the less useful it gets.

None of this is a flaw in TikTok. It's a video app, and Favorites is a video bookmark.

## What a recipe box needs

A place you'll actually cook from has:

- The ingredients as a list, with amounts.
- The steps in order, readable at a glance.
- A link back to the video for the bit you need to see.
- A copy you keep, that doesn't depend on the original staying up.
- Search, or at least groups that make sense, like "weeknight".

You can build that in Notes for free by copying captions. Or use a recipe app that does the copying.

## Use both

The setup that works for most people: keep using Favorites for videos, and move the ones you'll actually cook into a recipe box. When you see something you want to make this week, don't just tap the bookmark. Save the recipe.

## FAQ

**Q: Where are my TikTok Favorites?**
A: On your profile, under the Favorites tab (the bookmark icon). Collections you've made are inside Favorites too.

**Q: What happens to a favorited TikTok if the creator deletes it?**
A: It stops being playable from your Favorites. The recipe in its caption goes with it. If you want to keep a recipe, save the text somewhere you control.

**Q: Can I search my TikTok Favorites?**
A: Not by ingredient. Favorites shows saved videos as a grid you scroll, and collections help you group them. To search recipes by what's in them, they need to be saved as text.

**Q: Can ChatGPT go through my TikTok Favorites?**
A: No. It can't see your TikTok account. If you copy recipe captions into it, it can tidy them into ingredient lists and steps, but you'd still need somewhere to keep them.
