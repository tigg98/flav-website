# How do I cook from a TikTok without pausing the video every ten seconds?

Short answer: don't cook from the video. Before you start, get the recipe out of it as text, with the ingredients listed and the steps numbered, and cook from that. Watch the video once beforehand to see what the food should look like. Then put your phone where you can read it, and only go back to the video for the one step you can't picture.

We build Flav, an app with a Cook Mode for this. But the method matters more than the app.

## Why cooking from the video is so annoying

Short cooking videos are made to be watched, not followed. They're fast, they cut out the waiting, and amounts often flash on screen for a second or don't appear at all. Steps that take ten minutes in a real kitchen take two seconds on screen. So you pause, scrub back, unlock your phone with a greasy knuckle, and lose your place.

None of that is the creator's fault. The format just isn't a recipe.

## Before you cook: three things

**1. Watch it once, all the way through, before you touch anything.** Notice the moments that matter: what color the onions get, how thick the sauce is, when they turn the heat down. That's the part a written recipe can't give you.

**2. Get the recipe as text.** Copy the caption into a note, or share the TikTok to a recipe app that pulls out the ingredients and steps. If the caption is empty, check the comments and the creator's bio link. [This guide covers that](/guides/tiktok-recipe-not-in-caption).

**3. Get everything out first.** Measure the ingredients into bowls before you turn on the heat. Short videos almost always assume you have. Most of the pausing people do is "wait, how much soy sauce?" while something burns.

## While you cook

- **One step on screen at a time.** A long block of text is almost as hard to follow as the video. Read the step, do it, then look at the next.
- **Set timers from the recipe, not from memory.** "Simmer for 12 minutes" should be a timer the moment you start simmering.
- **Keep the screen awake.** Turn up Auto-Lock in Settings while you cook, or use an app that keeps the screen on in a cooking view.
- **Prop the phone up.** Somewhere you can read it from the stove, out of splash range.
- **Rewatch on purpose.** When you need the video, watch that one step. Then back to the text.

Fix the recipe while it's fresh. If it needed more salt, or the 12 minutes was really 18 on your stove, change it. The second time you cook it, you won't need the video at all.

## FAQ

**Q: How do I stop my phone locking while I cook?**
A: Go to Settings, Display and Brightness, Auto-Lock, and set it longer or to Never while you cook. Set it back after. Some recipe apps keep the screen on while you're in their cooking view.

**Q: Is it better to cook from the video or a written recipe?**
A: Cook from the written recipe and use the video as a reference. Watch it once before you start so you know what the food should look like, then follow the text one step at a time.

**Q: Can ChatGPT turn a TikTok into step-by-step instructions?**
A: If you paste in the caption, ChatGPT can number the steps and list the ingredients. It won't run timers or keep your place while you cook, and it can't reliably watch the video for you.

**Q: Does Flav's Cook Mode work with the screen locked?**
A: Timers keep running and show on the Lock Screen as a Live Activity, so you can glance at the countdown without unlocking. On Apple Watch, the current step and timer show on your wrist.
