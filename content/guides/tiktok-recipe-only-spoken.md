# How do I get the recipe from a TikTok when the creator only says it out loud?

Short answer: write it down once, from the talking, so you never have to do it again. Check that it isn't written somewhere else first. Then play the video twice, once for the ingredients and once for the steps, and mark every amount the creator never said as a guess. We make Flav, a recipe app for iPhone. When a TikTok has captions, Flav+ writes the recipe from what's said and marks the amounts that weren't said out loud as estimates.

## First, make sure it's really only spoken

Give it two minutes before you start writing. Read the whole caption, not just the first line, then look for a written version in the other places a creator might keep one. [This guide covers where to look](/guides/tiktok-recipe-not-in-caption).

A written recipe from the creator beats anything you take from the audio. It usually has the amounts, and it's the version they meant you to cook.

## Why a spoken recipe is hard to keep

Talking doesn't come in recipe order. The creator names each ingredient as it goes into the pan, not in a list at the start. Amounts turn into "some garlic" and "a good pour of oil". Waiting gets cut, so ten minutes of simmering is one sentence.

That's why one listen is never enough. You're doing two jobs, hearing it and sorting it, and it's easier to do them one at a time.

## Write it down in two passes

1. **Pass one, ingredients only.** Play the video through and write each ingredient as you hear it. If they say an amount, write it next to the ingredient. Ignore the steps for now.
2. **Pass two, steps only.** Play it again and number each thing the creator does, in order. Write down any heat or time they mention.
3. **Add the amounts you can see.** A whole can, three cloves, a full block of tofu. Put a question mark next to every number you guessed.
4. **Put the video's link at the top.** Text tells you what to do. The video shows you what it should look like.

Pause as often as you need. Nobody is timing you, and you only do this once per recipe.

If the words are hard to catch, captions help. Apple says Live Captions on iPhone shows captions for the audio in any app. Use captions to check what you heard, mostly the numbers, and trust your ears when the two disagree.

## Tidy it while it's fresh

Rough notes go stale fast. The same evening, put the ingredients in one list in the order you use them, and make each step one action. If your notes are a mess, you can paste them into an AI assistant and ask for an ingredient list and numbered steps. Tell it not to add amounts you didn't give it, and read the result against your notes.

## The amounts nobody said

Some amounts were never said and never shown. For those, the first cook is the measuring: start low on salt, chili and anything sour, taste, and write down what you used. [This guide goes through it](/guides/tiktok-recipe-with-no-measurements).
