# How do I save a recipe from TikTok?

Short answer: tapping Favorites saves the video, not the recipe. To keep the ingredients and steps, you have to get them out of the video and into something you can read in the kitchen. You can copy the caption into Notes by hand, or share the TikTok to a recipe app that pulls the recipe out for you. We make one of those apps, Flav, so this guide shows both ways.

## What you're actually trying to save

A recipe you can cook from has three parts: the ingredients with amounts, the steps in order, and a link back to the video so you can rewatch the one tricky bit. Favorites gives you the third part only. Every time you want to cook, you're back to scrubbing through a 60 second clip with flour on your hands.

So the job is to turn the video into text, once, and keep the link next to it.

## The free way: copy the caption

Most food creators put the recipe in the caption, or at least the ingredients.

1. Open the TikTok and tap the caption to expand it. Long captions are cut off with "more".
2. Press and hold the caption text and copy it. If TikTok won't let you select it, take a screenshot and use Live Text in Photos to copy the text from the image.
3. Paste it into a note. Add the video link at the top: tap Share, then Copy link.
4. Tidy it while it's fresh. Put the ingredients in a list and number the steps.

This works and costs nothing. The catch is step 4. Captions are written for scrolling, not cooking. Amounts sit in the middle of sentences, steps are out of order, and "season to taste" is doing a lot of work. Most people skip the tidying, and the note ends up as hard to cook from as the video.

## The quicker way: share it to a recipe app

A recipe app that understands TikTok links does the copying and the tidying. Here's how it goes in Flav:

1. On the TikTok, tap Share. TikTok's own panel comes up first. Tap More (or the share icon at the end of the row) to open the iPhone share sheet.
2. Pick Flav. The first time, you may need to scroll to the end of the app row, tap More, and add Flav to your favorites so it shows up next time.
3. Flav reads the caption and the page and saves a recipe: a title, the ingredients with quantities, and the steps in order, with the video link kept on the recipe.
4. Check it once. If the creator wrote "a splash of soy", that's what you'll see. Fix anything you want while you're there.

If you'd rather not use the share sheet, tap Copy link on the TikTok, open Flav, and paste it. Same result.

An import usually takes a few seconds. A very long caption can take about 15.

Imports are private. Nobody sees your cookbook unless you share it with your household.

## When the caption is empty

Some creators say the recipe out loud and write nothing down, or the caption just says "recipe on my page". A copy and paste gets you nothing, and so does an app that reads captions. Flav 2.0 reads the caption and the page, not the video itself, so on a video like that you'll get a thin recipe or an empty one. We'd rather tell you that than guess at amounts.

There are still ways to get it. [This guide covers them](/guides/tiktok-recipe-not-in-caption): the comments, the link in the creator's bio, and writing it down yourself while the video plays.

## A few habits that help

- Save it the day you see it. TikToks get deleted, made private, or lost in a Favorites list of 900 videos.
- Keep the link with the recipe. Text tells you what to do. The video shows you what "until it looks glossy" means.
- Fix amounts on the first cook. If you used two cloves of garlic instead of "some garlic", write that down. Next time it's your recipe.
- Don't save twenty at once. Save the ones you'd actually make this month. The rest are entertainment, and that's fine.

## FAQ

**Q: Does saving a TikTok to Favorites save the recipe?**
A: No. Favorites saves the video. The ingredients and steps stay inside the video and its caption, so you still have to watch it again to cook. To keep the recipe itself, copy the caption into a note or share the TikTok to a recipe app.

**Q: Can I save a TikTok recipe without an app?**
A: Yes. Expand the caption, copy the text (or screenshot it and copy with Live Text), and paste it into Notes with the video link. Tidy the ingredients into a list and number the steps. It's free, it just takes a few minutes per recipe.

**Q: Can ChatGPT get the recipe from a TikTok link?**
A: Sometimes. If you paste the caption text into ChatGPT, it can turn it into a clean ingredient list and steps. Whether it can open a TikTok link and read it depends on the assistant and the video. Either way it won't keep the recipe anywhere you'll find it when you're cooking next week.

**Q: What if the TikTok recipe isn't in the caption?**
A: Check the comments for a pinned recipe, and the creator's bio for a link to their site. If they only say it out loud, write it down while the video plays. Apps that read the caption and page, Flav 2.0 included, can't pull a recipe that was never written down.
