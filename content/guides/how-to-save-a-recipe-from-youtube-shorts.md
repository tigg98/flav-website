# How do I save a recipe from YouTube Shorts?

Short answer: on a Short, the recipe is usually in the description, not on screen, or in a longer video the Short links to. Open the description, copy the ingredients and steps into a note with the Short's link, or save the link to a recipe app that reads the page. Saving the Short to a YouTube playlist keeps the video, not the recipe.

We make Flav, a recipe app. Here's how to do it by hand first.

## Where the recipe is on a Short

Shorts hide the description. On the iPhone app, tap the three dots on the Short, then Description. That opens the text the creator wrote. Food creators often put the recipe there, or a link to it.

Check three places:

- **The description.** Ingredients, sometimes full steps, sometimes a link to their site.
- **A linked video.** Many Shorts link to a longer video from the same channel. Look for a link above the channel name or in the description. The full-length video's description usually has the complete recipe.
- **The comments.** Creators sometimes pin the recipe as a comment.

If it's on the creator's site, open that page. A recipe web page is the easiest kind to save, because it's written out with amounts and steps already.

## The free way

1. Open the Short's description and copy the recipe text. If you can't select it, screenshot it and copy the words with Live Text in Photos.
2. Tap Share on the Short and Copy link.
3. Paste both into a note, the link at the top.
4. Put the ingredients in a list and number the steps.

That gives you something to cook from that doesn't depend on finding the Short again.

## What a YouTube playlist does and doesn't do

Saving a Short to Watch Later or a playlist is fine for rewatching. But to cook you'd still open YouTube, find the Short, open the description, and scroll. And if the creator removes it, it's gone from your playlist. Keep the playlist for videos, and save the recipe somewhere else.

How well that works depends on what the creator wrote. A Short with the full recipe in the description comes through well. A Short whose description just says "full recipe on my channel" won't, because the recipe isn't on that page. In that case, open the longer video or the creator's site and save that link instead.

And if the recipe is only spoken in the Short, Flav 2.0 can't pull it out. It reads the page, not the video. You'll get a thin recipe or nothing. Reading the video itself is planned for a later version. Until then, watch it once with captions on and write it down.

## A couple of tips

- **Save the longer video, not the Short, when there is one.** It almost always has more in the description.
- **Check amounts.** Shorts move fast. If the description gives amounts and the video shows different ones, the description is usually right. Usually.
- **Keep the link.** Text tells you what to do. The video shows you when it's done.

## FAQ

**Q: Where is the description on a YouTube Short?**
A: On the iPhone app, tap the three dots on the Short and choose Description. The creator's text, including any recipe or links, is there.

**Q: Why can't I find the recipe in a YouTube Short?**
A: Short descriptions are often brief. Look for a link to a longer video from the same channel, check the pinned comment, and look for a link to the creator's website. The full recipe is usually in one of those.

**Q: Can ChatGPT get a recipe from a YouTube Short?**
A: Sometimes. If you paste the description in, it can tidy it into a list and steps. Whether it can open the link and read the video depends on the assistant. It won't keep the recipe for you to cook from later.

**Q: Does saving a Short to a playlist save the recipe?**
A: No. It saves the video. The recipe stays in the description, and it disappears with the video if the creator takes it down.
